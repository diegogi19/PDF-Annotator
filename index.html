IB Business Management Topics 1.1–1.4 Your Company, Your Decision What should your chosen business do next—and why? Team members: Your mission: Choose a real business, investigate how it operates, and recommend one decision. Apply topics 1.1–1.4 to your business instead of listing definitions. Work in teams of three. Choose your company and your question You may choose a local or international business, any industry, and a small or large organization. Select one with enough reliable information about its ownership, activities, and objectives. If you choose a brand, identify the company that owns it and state which entity you are studying. Choose a focused decision that interests you: changing packaging, raising prices, improving working conditions, changing suppliers, or another suitable idea. You may analyse a documented decision or propose a new one. Clearly label a proposed situation as hypothetical; do not present it as company news. Company: Our question: Should (company) ? 1.1 — What does this business do? • Identify its main goods or services and the customer need it meets. • Identify and justify its main economic sector: primary, secondary, tertiary, or quaternary. If it operates in several, explain your chosen focus. • Explain one entrepreneurial opportunity or challenge relevant to starting a business of this kind. Label your own inference clearly. 1.2 — How is it owned? • Identify its business entity type and whether it belongs to the private or public sector. Support the classification with evidence. • Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of this entity type for this business. 1.3 — What is it trying to achieve? • Explain two business objectives, using evidence where available. Distinguish confirmed objectives from your own reasoned inferences. • Connect one broad strategic objective to one proposed short-term tactical objective. Label your proposed objective clearly. • Explain one ethical or corporate social responsibility (CSR) consideration linked to your chosen decision. Consider a possible cost or trade-off. 1.4 — Who is affected? Create a table or stakeholder map with four relevant groups, including internal and external stakeholders. For each, identify its interest and explain how your decision could affect it. Analyse one conflict between two groups: explain why their interests differ. 1 IB Business Management Topics 1.1–1.4 Make and defend your recommendation Answer your question with a clear recommendation. Compare it with one realistic alternative, which may be keeping the current approach. Explain a benefit, a drawback, and a stakeholder trade-off. Identify one limitation in your evidence and explain how it makes your conclusion less certain. Choose your format Submit one of these, plus your source list: • Up to 8 content slides; or • A 4-page business brief; or • A poster, with up to one page of supporting notes. All formats must cover the four topic sections and your recommendation. A simple, clear design can earn full marks. No separate long report is required. Sources: Use at least three relevant sources, including one official company source and one independent source. Cite facts near the claim using [1], [2], etc. List the author/organization, title, date if available, URL, and access date. Company claims are evidence of what it says, not automatic proof of success. Do not invent ownership details, figures, or objectives. If an important fact cannot be verified, state the uncertainty or choose a better-documented company. AI output is not a source: verify claims against the originals. Present: 4 minutes + 2 minutes of questions Everyone speaks and understands the whole project. Suggested roles: business and ownership; objectives and stakeholders; recommendation and trade-offs. The teacher may ask any member to explain a source, defend a claim, or respond to a changed situation.
